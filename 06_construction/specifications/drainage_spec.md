## 1. GENERAL

### 1.1 Scope of Works

This Specification governs all surface water drainage works for the playgrounds at the six (6)
SUIDAC schools. The Works comprise, without limitation:

1. Formation of graded surfaces to the minimum falls specified.
2. Construction of drainage swales to the standard 300 × 300 mm section.
3. Supply and placement of rubble / coarse gravel swale fill.
4. Sub-drainage of sand fall zones and sand pits.
5. Formation of infiltration sumps / soakaways where shown.
6. Construction of outfalls, energy dissipation and erosion protection.
7. Crossings where pathways intersect swales.
8. Wet-season protection, commissioning by flood test and handover of the maintenance regime.

The drainage system is **gravity-based only — no pumps** (Master Command §4.6).

## 2. PRODUCTS AND MATERIALS

### 2.1 Swale Fill — Rubble / Coarse Gravel

| Property | Requirement |
|----------|-------------|
| Material | Rubble or coarse gravel (Master Command §4.3) |
| Depth in swale | **200 mm** (Master Command §4.3) |
| Nominal size | 40–100 mm, hard, sound, durable rock [E] |
| Fines | ≤ 5 % passing 5 mm; material shall be free-draining and not clay-bound [E] |
| Shape | Angular preferred, for interlock and flow resistance [E] |
| Source | **Local quarry within 50 km of Assosa** (Master Command §4.3, §4.4) |
| Prohibited | Demolition rubble containing reinforcement, timber, glass, plastic or sharp fragments accessible to children [E] |

### 2.2 Filter / Separation Layer

To prevent migration of fines and loss of void space [E]:

| Option | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| Graded granular filter | 50 mm layer of 10–20 mm clean gravel between the rubble and the subgrade / sand |
| Geotextile | Non-woven separation geotextile, ≥ 150 g/m², where locally available within the 50 km sourcing radius; **imported specialty products are not permitted** (Master Command §4.5) |

Where neither is available, an inverted graded filter shall be built up from locally available
gravel sizes to the Engineer's approval [E].

### 2.3 Sub-Drainage Materials for Sand Zones

| Item | Requirement |
|------|-------------|
| Drainage blanket | 50–100 mm of 10–20 mm clean gravel beneath the sand body, laid on the 1 % graded formation [E] |
| Rubble sump | 300 × 300 × 300 mm minimum, filled with 40–100 mm rubble, at the low point of each sand zone [E] |
| Separation | Filter layer or geotextile between the sand and the drainage blanket, to Clause 2.2 [E] |

### 2.4 Erosion Protection and Outfall Materials

Stone pitching or rubble apron material, 100–200 mm nominal size, hard and sound, bedded on a
granular or C20 concrete bed as detailed [E]. Concrete, where used at outfalls and crossings, shall
be **hand-mixed C20** in accordance with SUIDAC-SPEC-03 Clause 2.4 (Master Command §4.6).

## 3. EXECUTION

### 3.1 General Principles

1. All drainage shall operate **by gravity only; no pumps of any kind** (Master Command §4.6).
2. All construction shall be executed by **manual labour with basic tools — no heavy machinery**
   (Master Command §4.6).
3. Every surface shall be graded to fall to a positive outlet; **no closed depressions, flat spots
   or reverse falls are permitted** [E].
4. The system shall be designed and constructed so that **all surfaces drain within 24 hours**
   (Master Command §4.4).
5. Drainage shall not discharge onto neighbouring property, into classroom foundations, or into any
   area where children congregate [E].

### 3.2 Surface Falls

1. All graded surfaces shall be constructed to a **minimum fall of 1 %** (Master Command §5 D3), to
   the spot levels, gradients and flow directions shown on the `grading_drainage` drawing.
2. Recommended working gradients [E], all of which satisfy the 1 % minimum:

   | Surface | Gradient |
   |---------|----------|
   | Gravel pathways (cross-fall) | 1.0 % minimum – 2.0 % maximum |
   | Gravel pathways (longitudinal) | 1.0 % minimum – 4.0 % maximum |
   | Sand pit formation beneath sand | 1.0 % minimum – 2.0 % maximum, falling to the sump |
   | General play lawn | 1.0 % minimum – 2.5 % maximum |
   | Quiet / shade zones | 1.0 % minimum – 2.5 % maximum |
   | Ground adjacent to buildings, falling away | 2.0 % minimum over the first 1.5 m [E] |

3. Falls shall be set out and checked with a dumpy level or a water level; eye-judged grading is
   not acceptable [E].
4. Finished falls shall be verified after compaction, since settlement can create low spots
   (SUIDAC-SPEC-01 Clause 3.8).

### 3.3 Swale Construction

1. Swales shall be constructed to the **standard 300 × 300 mm section**
   (Master Command §5 D3), as detailed on `DWG_drainage_swale_section.dwg` and set out on the
   `grading_drainage` drawing.
2. **Standard section build-up:**

   | Element | Dimension |
   |---------|-----------|
   | Nominal excavated section | **300 mm wide × 300 mm deep** (Master Command) |
   | Side batters, where formed in open play areas | 1 : 1 minimum, flattened to 1 : 2 where the swale crosses a circulation route [E] |
   | Filter / separation layer | 50 mm graded gravel or geotextile [E] |
   | **Rubble / coarse gravel fill** | **200 mm** (Master Command §4.3) |
   | Freeboard above the rubble to finished ground | **100 mm** — being 300 mm depth less 200 mm fill [E, arithmetic consequence of the two specified values] |

3. **Longitudinal gradient:** not less than the **1 %** minimum. Where the available gradient
   exceeds 4 %, provide rubble check dams at maximum 10 m centres to limit scour velocity [E].
4. **Alignment:** swales shall follow the alignments shown, generally along the low side of the
   play area and along pathway edges, and shall discharge to the outfall points shown.
5. **Exclusion zones — mandatory:**
   - No swale shall be routed through, or encroach upon, the **2.0 m clear fall zone** of any
     moving equipment (Master Command §4.1);
   - No swale shall be routed through the **8 m × 3 m** E2 See-Saw safety zone or the E3 slide
     runout;
   - Swales shall be kept clear of the **1.5 m** general setback and the **3.0 m** swing / slide
     setback wherever this can be achieved without compromising falls [E].
6. **Child safety:** the maximum open depth of any swale shall be **300 mm**; deeper open channels,
   vertical sides and open pits are not permitted anywhere within the play area
   [E — consequence of the specified standard section].
7. Swale invert and sides shall be trimmed by hand, compacted, and free of loose material before the
   filter layer and rubble are placed.
8. Rubble shall be hand-placed, not tipped, to avoid damaging the filter layer, and shall be
   finished so that no stone stands proud of the adjacent ground surface [E].

### 3.4 Sand Pit and Fall Zone Sub-Drainage

Sand fall zones — **150–200 mm** deep beneath moving equipment and **300 mm** at the slide
runout — must not become saturated bowls; sand retains water and fails the 24-hour drain-down
requirement if the base is impermeable [E].

1. Grade the sand-pit formation to a **1 % minimum fall** to a designated low corner.
2. Lay a filter / separation layer, then a **50–100 mm drainage blanket** of 10–20 mm clean gravel
   over the formation [E].
3. At the low point, construct a **rubble sump of 300 × 300 × 300 mm minimum**, filled with
   40–100 mm rubble and wrapped in filter material [E].
4. Where the ground allows, connect the sump by a stone-filled trench, laid at not less than 1 %, to
   the nearest swale [E]. Where no gravity connection is achievable, the sump shall be enlarged to
   function as a soakaway to Clause 3.5.
5. Sump and trench works shall be entirely below the sand; **no rubble, stone or hard edge shall be
   exposed within any fall zone** (see SUIDAC-SPEC-02 and SUIDAC-SPEC-03).

### 3.5 Infiltration Sumps / Soakaways

Where shown on the drawings, or where no gravity outfall exists [E]:

1. Hand-excavate a soakaway pit of minimum plan 600 × 600 mm and minimum depth 900 mm, sited not
   less than 5 m from any building foundation and not less than 2 m from any boundary or fence line.
2. Fill with 40–100 mm rubble, wrapped in filter material, and cap with 150 mm of compacted
   granular material and topsoil / grass.
3. Confirm suitability by a simple falling-head infiltration test: a test pit filled with water
   shall show a drop of not less than 100 mm in 24 hours. Where the lateritic subgrade fails this
   test, the soakaway shall be deepened, enlarged, or a gravity connection to a swale provided.
4. Soakaways shall not be located within any **2.0 m fall zone** or beneath any equipment footing.

### 3.6 Outfalls and Discharge

1. Swales shall discharge to the existing site drainage, an approved external road-side drain, or an
   on-site infiltration feature, as shown on the `grading_drainage` drawing.
2. **Discharge onto neighbouring property, footpaths outside the compound or classroom aprons is
   prohibited** [E].
3. Provide a stone-pitched or rubble energy-dissipation apron at every outfall, minimum
   1.0 m × 1.0 m, to prevent scour [E].
4. Where a swale discharges through the school fence line, provide a hand-formed opening protected
   with a stone-pitched channel and a coarse rubble screen that will not trap children or refuse
   [E].
5. Silt shall be intercepted upstream of the outfall by a rubble silt trap, accessible for hand
   cleaning [E].

### 3.7 Pathway and Circulation Crossings

1. Where a **1.2 m minimum width** pathway (Master Command §4.1) crosses a swale, provide a hand-cast
   C20 concrete slab crossing or a short concrete culvert, with the walking surface flush with the
   adjacent path [E].
2. Crossings shall carry the full path width, shall have no step or upstand exceeding 20 mm, and
   shall maintain the swale's hydraulic section beneath [E].
3. Culverts shall be a minimum of 200 mm internal diameter, laid at not less than 1 %, with headwalls
   and inlet / outlet protection [E]; where the L3 tunnel-crawl culvert type is re-used as a drainage
   culvert, it shall be sized and detailed as drainage, never as a play element.
4. Crossings shall be accessible for hand cleaning; blocked crossings are the most common cause of
   upstream ponding [E].

### 3.8 Protection of Buildings, Trees and Timber

1. Ground adjacent to classroom walls shall fall **away** from the building over the first 1.5 m,
   maintaining the **1.5 m** general setback as a positively drained margin (Master Command §4.1) [E].
2. Swales and infiltration features shall not be located within existing tree protection rings
   (3–5 m radius) without the Engineer's approval [E].
3. No timber or bamboo element shall be located where water stands after the 24-hour drain-down
   period (SUIDAC-SPEC-04 Clause 3.7).

### 3.9 Wet-Season Working and Temporary Drainage

1. Permanent drainage shall be constructed **early in the sequence**, ahead of surfacing, so that
   the works area is drained throughout construction [E].
2. During the wet season (**April–October**), provide temporary cut-off drains upslope of open
   excavations, discharging to the permanent swale system [E].
3. Silt generated during construction shall be trapped and removed; swales shall be cleaned of
   construction silt before handover.
4. During the dry season (**November–March**), bare swale batters shall be dampened for dust
   suppression and grassed as soon as practicable (Master Command §4.4).

### 3.10 Tolerances

| Item | Tolerance |
|------|-----------|
| Swale width | **+50 mm / −0 mm** on 300 mm [E] |
| Swale depth | **+50 mm / −0 mm** on 300 mm [E] |
| Rubble fill depth | ± 20 mm on 200 mm [E] |
| Freeboard above rubble | 100 mm nominal, ± 20 mm [E] |
| Swale longitudinal gradient | Not less than the **1 %** minimum, at every point along the invert |
| Invert level | ± 15 mm from design, provided the minimum gradient is maintained [E] |
| Surface fall on graded areas | Not less than **1 %**; no reverse fall permitted |
| Crossing step / upstand | 20 mm maximum [E] |

Negative tolerance on any Master Command minimum value — 1 % fall, 300 mm section, 200 mm rubble —
is not permitted.

## 4. QUALITY ASSURANCE, TESTING AND COMMISSIONING

### 4.1 Submittals

| Submittal | Timing |
|-----------|--------|
| Rubble / coarse gravel source and haul distance (≤ 50 km), with sample | 14 days before delivery [E] |
| Filter material or geotextile proposal | Before swale construction [E] |
| Infiltration test results for each proposed soakaway | Before soakaway construction |
| As-built swale invert levels and gradients | Before rubble placement |
| Flood test / drain-down records | At commissioning |
| Outfall agreement with the adjacent landowner or city authority, where applicable | Before outfall construction [E] |

### 4.2 Inspection and Testing

| Check | Frequency |
|-------|-----------|
| Swale section dimensions (300 × 300 mm) | 1 per 10 m of swale, minimum 3 per school [E] |
| Swale gradient by level survey (≥ 1 %) | Continuous string of levels at 5 m intervals [E] |
| Rubble fill depth (200 mm) by probe | 1 per 10 m of swale [E] |
| Surface fall check on all graded areas | Every panel, before surfacing |
| Ponding check after rainfall | Every wet-season rainfall event during construction [E] |
| Sand zone sub-drain and sump construction check | 100 % of zones, before sand placement |
| Infiltration (falling head) test | Every soakaway |
| Outfall scour protection check | 100 % of outfalls |

> **HOLD POINT 7 — Swale invert.** Rubble shall not be placed until the invert level, gradient and
> filter layer have been inspected and released.

### 4.3 Commissioning — 24-Hour Drain-Down Test

The performance requirement of Master Command §4.4 shall be demonstrated, not assumed:

1. **Method [E]:** in the absence of natural rainfall, flood each surfaced zone and each swale reach
   with water applied evenly at a rate representative of a heavy storm — a practical site test is
   the application of 40 litres per square metre over the test area, or a natural rainfall event
   exceeding 10 mm.
2. **Pass criterion:** **no standing water anywhere on the site 24 hours after the end of the
   application** (Master Command §4.4). Sand zones shall be free-draining and shall not be
   waterlogged at 24 hours; the sand shall be workable by hand rake.
3. Record the test date, method, quantity applied, weather, and the condition of every zone at
   1 hour, 4 hours and 24 hours.
4. Any zone that fails the test shall be re-graded, the sub-drain enlarged, or the swale connection
   improved, and shall be re-tested until it passes. **Failure of the 24-hour criterion in any zone
   is a bar to handover.**

### 4.4 Non-Conformance

Reverse falls, flat spots, blocked crossings, silted swales, under-depth rubble and swales
encroaching on fall zones shall be recorded as non-conformances and rectified before handover.

## 5. COMPLETION AND MAINTENANCE

### 5.1 Handover Criteria

1. All graded surfaces achieve a **minimum 1 % fall** to a positive gravity outlet.
2. All swales constructed to the **300 × 300 mm** standard section with **200 mm** rubble fill and
   100 mm freeboard.
3. All swale gradients **≥ 1 %**, continuous to the outfall, with no ponding along the invert.
4. Every sand zone provided with a graded formation, drainage blanket and sump; no exposed rubble
   within any fall zone.
5. No swale, sump or soakaway within any **2.0 m fall zone**.
6. All outfalls protected against scour, discharging to an agreed and lawful destination.
7. All pathway crossings flush, clear and hand-cleanable, maintaining the **1.2 m** path width.
8. **24-hour drain-down test passed for every zone**, with records submitted.
9. All construction silt removed; swale batters grassed or otherwise protected.
10. **No pumps or powered drainage components anywhere in the system.**

### 5.3 Maintenance Regime (for handover to School Maintenance Committee)

| Task | Frequency | Standard |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Pre-wet-season clean-out** — clear all swales, sumps, crossings and outfalls of silt, sand, leaves and refuse | **Annually in March**, before the April onset of the wet season | Full 200 mm rubble depth and 100 mm freeboard restored |
| Inspect swales and outfalls after heavy rainfall | Each significant storm during April–October [E] | No blockage, no scour, no overtopping |
| Rake and wash silt from the rubble surface; replace clogged rubble | Annually, or when the rubble is visibly silted [E] | Rubble free-draining |
| Check and reinstate the **1 % minimum fall** where settlement or scour has occurred | Annually [E] | No ponding after rainfall |
| Clear pathway crossings and culverts | Monthly during the wet season [E] | Free flow through full section |
| Check sand zones for waterlogging after rainfall | After each significant storm [E] | Sand drains within 24 hours |
| Repair scour at outfalls; reinstate stone apron | As required [E] | No progressive erosion |
| Re-grass swale batters | Annually, at the start of the wet season [E] | Continuous cover, no bare erodible soil |
| Dust suppression on bare drainage areas | As required, **Nov–Mar** | No airborne dust in play areas |

The school shall be advised in the handover briefing that **the pre-wet-season clean-out in March is
the single most important maintenance task**: a silted swale will cause ponding in the play area and
loss of the 24-hour drain-down performance within one season.
